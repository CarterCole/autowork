# Guide for Volunteers

For people who want to lend their AI assistant's time to a charity's or a
project's queue.

## What you are doing

An organisation keeps a public list of work it would like done. You point your
own AI coding assistant at that list. It picks a piece of work, does it, and
sends the result back for the organisation to judge. You are donating your
assistant's effort and a few minutes of your own attention.

## What you need

- A GitHub account.
- An AI coding assistant that can run commands: VS Code with Copilot, Claude
  Code, Codex, or similar.
- git and the GitHub command-line tool. Your assistant will tell you if either
  is missing and how to get it.

## What it costs you

Your own AI use, on whatever plan you have. One work item is roughly one
working session for your assistant. You can stop at any point. Nothing here
charges you, and the queue never asks for payment details.

## Start

Run the install line from the queue's front page, or clone the repository.
Open the folder in your assistant and say:

> Read program.md and follow it.

It checks your setup, shows you the open work, and takes one item.

## What happens under your name

Three things, all public, all on GitHub:

1. A short comment on the work item, saying you have claimed it.
2. A copy ("fork") of the queue's repository in your account, the first time.
3. A pull request containing the finished work.

Your assistant is told to make sure you are happy with that before the first
one. It is also told what it must never do for a work item: touch anything
outside the queue's folder on your computer, look for or use passwords and
keys, install software, create accounts, spend money, or contact anyone.

## Stay in control

- **Ask to see the work first.** Say "show me the work before you submit it".
  Read it. Your name goes on it.
- **One item at a time** is the default. Your assistant stops and reports
  after each one unless you tell it to keep going.
- **Be honest about review.** Each submission records which assistant and
  model did the work and whether a person looked it over. Say what is true.
- **You can back out.** "Release the work item" gives your place back.

## Choose a queue you can trust

You are letting someone else's instructions direct your assistant, within the
limits above. Before you start on a new queue, read its `program.md` (it is
short) and a few of its work items. Work items can only be written by the
queue's maintainers. If one asks for something outside the limits, your
assistant is told to stop and tell you. If that happens, do not carry on.

## Afterwards

The organisation compares the submissions for each item and keeps the best.
You get a comment on your pull request either way:

- **Accepted.** Your work is merged, with your GitHub name in the history.
- **Not accepted.** You get the reasons. If the item is still open you may
  try again with those fixed.

Sometimes parts of a declined submission are carried into the final result,
with credit.

## Be a good guest

- Do not take more items than you will finish.
- Do not submit work you would be embarrassed to have reviewed.
- Never include personal or confidential information, yours or anyone's.
- Say where anything you did not write came from.
